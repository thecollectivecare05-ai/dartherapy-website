What to Expect in Your Child's First ABA Session

By DARTherapy Staff · October 17, 2025 · 3 min read

The first ABA session is mostly about connection. Rather than hard work, it's a chance for your child and their therapist to get comfortable, have fun, and start building trust — the foundation everything else is built on.

Key takeaways

  • The first session is about building rapport, not testing your child.
  • Expect play, observation and getting comfortable.
  • Your therapist is learning what your child enjoys and responds to.
  • There's no 'right' way for your child to behave — it's a relaxed start.
  • You can help by staying calm and positive.

The first session

Early sessions usually look a lot like play. The therapist follows your child's lead, joins in with favourite activities, and gently observes how your child communicates and responds. This 'pairing' builds the trust that makes learning possible.

There's no pressure for your child to perform. The goal is simply for them to feel safe and start to enjoy their time together.

How you can help

Before the session, keep things low-key and positive. Share useful details with the therapist — your child's favourite toys, what soothes them, and any words or signals they use.

Afterwards, celebrate that they tried something new. Every session builds on the last.
